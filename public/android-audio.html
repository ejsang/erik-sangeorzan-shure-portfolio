Build, deploy,
and inspect

The work combined source changes, image deployment, vendor configuration, and physical audio paths.

I created an Android source workspace, selected a build target, and built customized images incorporating native C instrumentation and audio configuration changes. Linux, ADB, and Fastboot were part of the build/deploy/debug cycle.

I added debug output to existing native C audio-test code, compiled and deployed the modified utility, and investigated runtime library dependency and search-path behavior. When vendor partition access blocked configuration updates, I used the rebuild/flash workflow to incorporate the changes.

I worked in an Android/QNX environment, including QNX image workflows and supplier-provided changes. My audio work included investigating existing Audio HAL/PAL startup and playback behavior and modifying AudioReach/QACT graphs, routing, and slot mappings.

A generic view of the investigation

Follow the signal across boundaries

When software success produced no sound

A successful command was one observation. Audible output needed separate validation.

  1. Make native execution observable

    I used debug instrumentation and logs to inspect the existing utility and investigate Audio HAL/PAL startup and playback. Runtime dependency behavior was a separate layer to check before interpreting the audio result.

  2. Check the configured path

    I examined vendor audio configuration and changed AudioReach/QACT graph routing and slot mappings. The investigation included A2B/TDM paths, PCM parameters, and ADC/DAC capture/playback.

  3. Separate digital evidence from physical output

    In a playback investigation, I used QXDM capture and QCAT audio reconstruction to help isolate a failure where software-level success did not produce sound. Supplier collaboration helped validate working physical output paths.

  4. Exercise capture as its own path

    I reconfigured a capture graph, rebuilt and flashed it, tested ADC paths, and documented working capture procedures. Playback success alone was insufficient evidence of recording success.

  5. Leave a repeatable procedure

    I worked with supplier engineers through support exchanges and suggested changes, and produced documentation and demonstrations to transfer the build, configuration, and validation workflow.

Results & scope

Successful playback and recording

Audio playback and recording were demonstrated on development hardware and custom IVI hardware. The outcome connects customized software images and graph/configuration work to a physical audio result.

Bounded validation

The result applies to the tested paths and configurations. Some concurrent-playback failures and supplier blockers remained unresolved.
